# Worked example — LifeguardPortal

The reference case study Johann approved. Use it as a model for tone, density, and how much specificity to aim for. Don't copy phrasing; copy the *level of detail*.

## Hero

> LifeguardPortal is the client portal for Lifeguard Digital Health's overdose-prevention technology. I led brand, web, and campaign design for its first public launch.

Two sentences: what the product is, what Johann did. Note it says what the product *actually* is — the earlier draft said "workforce and credential-management platform," which was invented from the product name.

Target ~18 words. In PP Mori at 66px across 1141px this wraps to four lines, matching the reference frame.

## Meta block

Role · Scope · Client · Year · Tools. Labels in the template render as `(scope)` / `(role)` / `(year)` — keep the parenthetical label style and put values beneath, don't overwrite labels with values.

## Results

Stat row:

| Webinar invites across two sessions | RSVP accepts | Blended acceptance rate | Assets shipped across five channels |

> Session one drew 14 accepts from 64 invites; session two drew 16 from 65 — a blended ~23% acceptance rate on a first-ever event with no CRM, no historical benchmark, and no existing marketing list to draw from.

Followed by a separated caveat:

> **Stated honestly:** attendance exceeded RSVPs because invites were forwarded beyond the original list. Good for the room, bad for the data — and the clearest argument for the tooling fix noted in the reflection.

## Overview

**Introduction** — who signs in and what they see. Names the real users (health authorities, shelters, supervised consumption sites, construction organizations) and the real metrics (alerts triggered, response times, confirmed interventions).

**Challenge** — the four products under one login, listed as a bulleted inventory:

- **LifeguardLite** — room-based overdose and environmental detection in shelters and supervised consumption sites
- **LifeguardConnect** — a mobile harm-reduction app for community and outreach settings
- **BuildStrong** — workplace wellness for CIRP construction members
- **Admin** — an internal cross-product view for Lifeguard staff

Then the actual difficulty: explaining life-safety software to three very different buyer types at once, with no CRM and no prior playbook.

**Solution** — the organizing idea, stated as a question every asset had to answer: *what does this let you see, and who does it protect?*

## Strategy

**Positioning pillars** as a table — pillar, the headline actually used, the benefit:

| Pillar | Headline | Benefit |
|---|---|---|
| Unified access | "One Login, Full Visibility" | One secure login replaces fragmented access |
| Real-time data | "No Delays On Your Data" | Near real-time updates |
| Reporting | "Exportable Reports" | Clear, shareable reports on demand |
| Enablement | "Marketing Materials Library" | Clients self-serve communications |

Real headline copy pulled from the Figma scope file — not invented. This is what makes the section credible.

**Audience segments** — the actual named customers (BC Emergency Health Services, Métis Nation BC, NorthWest Ontario, Lambton County) and the role-scoping reality (a manager sees org-level data, floor staff see only their building). That detail is what makes the government / non-government webinar split read as a considered decision rather than a scheduling convenience.

**Goals** — including the constraint that reframes everything: access is invite-only with staff-controlled permissions, so the campaign had to drive *qualified conversations rather than signups*. There is no self-registration funnel to optimize. Hiring managers notice this kind of constraint-awareness.

## Planning

Release plan as a dated table — Date / Phase / Channel / Deliverable — running from the March 4 teaser through the April 14–15 webinar to the weekly post-event social cadence. Real dates from the campaign calendar, not "week 1 / week 2."

## Deliverables

Opens with the count: *"roughly twenty assets spanning five channels."*

Then per channel — Website, Email System, Social Campaign, Webinar Campaign, Print & Onboarding — a paragraph of what was made and why, followed by a full-width image.

The details that carry weight:

- The email section explains *why* transactional emails were treated as brand work: access is invite-only, so the invite email is many users' first impression of the product.
- Named artifacts: the "LifeguardPortal Quick Start Guide," double-sided, also a downloadable PDF.
- Real subject line: "You've Been Invited to Join LifeguardPortal™"
- Formats: 1080×1350 social, desktop-first email with mobile variants.

## Reflection

Three paragraphs, no metrics (those live in Results):

1. What made it hard — first-ever webinar, building the plan and the process simultaneously, no playbook to inherit.
2. What went wrong, framed as fixable tooling problems rather than personal failure — no CRM, invite-forwarding, and what he'd do differently.
3. What he'd keep — leading with outcomes over features, and why that matters specifically for a product reporting on overdose alerts.

## Things that were wrong in earlier drafts

Worth knowing what to avoid:

- **Invented product description** from the name alone. Fixed only after reading the 32-page product overview PDF.
- **Metrics buried in Reflection**, framed inside a paragraph about what went wrong — made a respectable first-campaign number read as a failure.
- **No deliverables section at all**, because the draft was edited inside a cloned template with a fixed number of slots and there was nowhere to add one.
- **Generic invented personas** ("Building Managers, Authorized Partners, Care Homes" used as if they were researched segments) instead of the real named customers in the source material.
