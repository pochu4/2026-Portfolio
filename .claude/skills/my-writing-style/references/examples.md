# Worked examples

Read this when a first draft feels off, or when the request is a situation SKILL.md doesn't obviously cover. Each example shows a generic-sounding draft next to Johann's version, with a note on what actually changed.

## Rewrites: generic to Johann

### Asking someone for something

**Generic:**
> Hi Marcus,
>
> I hope you're doing well. I wanted to reach out regarding the analytics access we discussed in last week's meeting. When you have a moment, could you please advise on the timeline for provisioning? This is currently blocking our reporting workstream.
>
> Thank you in advance for your assistance.
>
> Best regards,
> Johann

**Johann:**
> Hi Marcus,
>
> Following up on the analytics access from last week. Any idea when that might come through? I'm holding off on the reporting work until I can get in, so it'd be good to know if it's days or weeks.
>
> No rush if it's stuck on something, just let me know either way.
>
> Thanks,
> Johann

What changed: the windup is gone, "advise on the timeline" became "any idea when", "blocking our reporting workstream" became a plain statement of what he's actually waiting on. The last line does the softening work without weakening the ask.

### Delivering bad news to a client

**Generic:**
> Hi Priya,
>
> Unfortunately we have encountered an issue with the integration that will impact our delivery timeline. Our engineering team is actively working to resolve the matter and we will provide an update as soon as we have further clarity. We apologize for any inconvenience this may cause.

**Johann:**
> Hi Priya,
>
> Bad news on the integration. Their API changed last week without notice and it broke the sync we'd built, so we're going to need a few extra days to rework it.
>
> Realistically that puts us at the 22nd instead of the 18th. I know that's not what you wanted to hear, and I'd rather tell you now than on the 17th.
>
> I'll have a firmer date for you by Wednesday once the team's had a proper look.
>
> Thanks,
> Johann

What changed: the vague "an issue" became the actual cause. "We will provide an update as soon as we have further clarity" became a specific commitment with a day attached. The apology got replaced with something more useful, which is acknowledging the impact and explaining why he's telling them early. Johann does not over-apologize, but he does not pretend the problem is smaller than it is either.

### Saying no

**Generic:**
> Thank you for thinking of me for this. Unfortunately, due to current bandwidth constraints, I don't believe I'm able to take this on at this time. I'd be happy to revisit in the future should circumstances change.

**Johann:**
> Thanks for asking, but I'm going to pass on this one. I've got the Q3 report and the vendor migration both landing in the same two weeks and I don't think I'd do a good job on top of that.
>
> If it's still open in September I'd be up for it.

What changed: the reason is real and specific instead of "bandwidth constraints." The offer to revisit is concrete rather than a polite formality. Note that he says "I don't think I'd do a good job" rather than "I don't have time." That's the honest reason and it's more respectful of the asker.

### A Slack message that got over-formalised

**Generic:**
> Hi team, just wanted to circle back on the action items from this morning's sync. Please find below a summary of ownership and next steps.

**Johann:**
> quick recap from this morning so nothing gets lost:
>
> - i'm redoing the pricing slide, done by thursday
> - priya's checking whether legal needs to see the contract language
> - marcus is chasing the analytics access
>
> shout if i got any of that wrong

What changed: lowercase, no "circle back", no "please find below." Bullets are used here because it genuinely is a list of parallel items, which is the right reason to use them. The closing line is an actual invitation to correct him, not a sign-off.

### A status update nobody will read

**Generic:**
> ## Executive Summary
> This document provides an overview of the current status of the onboarding redesign initiative, including key milestones achieved, outstanding risks, and recommended next steps for stakeholder consideration.

**Johann:**
> Where the onboarding redesign is at, as of today.
>
> The build is done and in QA. We're on track for the 14th unless QA turns up something big, which I don't expect but can't rule out yet.
>
> The one open risk is the email templates. They need sign-off from brand and I haven't heard back in a week. If that slips past Friday it starts eating into the launch buffer, so I'm going to chase it tomorrow.

What changed: the summary-of-a-summary opener is gone. Each paragraph carries actual information. The risk section has a named consequence and a named action instead of "outstanding risks."

## Situations that trip people up

**When the request is genuinely formal.** Legal notices, formal complaints, HR matters, anything with a compliance flavour. Write it properly, but hold the line on em dashes and empty phrases. Formal does not mean padded. Tell Johann you went more formal than usual and why, so he isn't surprised.

**When he pastes a message and says "reply to this."** Match the register of the incoming message, then apply his voice within it. A one-line Slack message gets a one-line reply. A formal client email gets the client register. Do not reply to a two-sentence message with four paragraphs.

**When he asks for something "professional."** He does not mean stiff. He means the client register: clean sentences, no lowercase, no slang, contractions still fine. If a draft starts sounding like a contract, it has overshot.

**When the topic is bad news, a mistake, or an apology.** Own it plainly and move to what happens next. Johann does not spiral into apology, and he does not minimise. One clear acknowledgement, then the fix.

**When there is nothing interesting to say.** Some messages are just logistics. Don't manufacture warmth or reasoning that isn't there. "Moved the review to Thursday, calendar's updated" is a complete message.

**When he asks for options.** Give two or three genuinely different versions rather than three shades of the same thing, and label what makes each different. He picks fast when the differences are real.

## Phrase swaps

Reach for the right column.

| Avoid | Use |
| --- | --- |
| I hope this email finds you well | (nothing, start with the point) |
| I just wanted to reach out | (nothing, or "Following up on...") |
| Let's sync / touch base / circle back | Let's talk / I'll follow up |
| Do you have bandwidth for | Do you have time for / Can you take on |
| Please advise | Let me know / What do you think |
| Per my last email | (restate the thing plainly, no passive aggression) |
| At your earliest convenience | When you get a chance / by Friday if possible |
| Please do not hesitate to contact me | Let me know if anything's unclear |
| Leverage | Use |
| Align on | Agree on / get on the same page about |
| Deliverable / workstream | The report / the project / the thing itself |
| We apologize for any inconvenience | (acknowledge the specific impact instead) |
| Thank you in advance | Thanks |
| I would like to inform you that | (delete, just say the thing) |
| It has come to my attention that | I noticed / I heard |
| Going forward | From now on / next time |
| Attached please find | I've attached / Here's |
| Kindly | Please, or nothing |
